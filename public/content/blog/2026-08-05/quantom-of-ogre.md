### On Free Will
So I've just finished playing *007: First Light* (heartily recommend) and it struck me that it's a beautiful example of the utility of the Quantum Ogre.  This is a thought-experiment/GMing tool and if you're not familiar with it, it goes like this;

```
There's two doors, and the players have to pick one.  Which ever one they pick has an ogre behind it, irrespective of any other choices they make.
```

It's a bit reductionist, but it highlights that it's okay to have linear experiences if you don't *know* that they're linear: the experience is that of having free will even if behind the scenes you don't.  And in a situation where you're experiencing a story it just *doesn't matter*.  It seems harsh but it often means you can focus more on the quality of a limited path through a story/game/whatever and don't have to worry about the players going off in totally the wrong direction - ultimately the result is the same.  

*007: First Light* is similar (maybe spoilers?).  It's a fundementally linear experience but it gives you enough of a feeling of freedom that it honestly feels like you're playing something a lot more open ended, along the lines of the *Hitman* games.  But because it *isn't*, the devs can polish the pacing and the tempo of the played experience to a much greater degree: because they give the sensation of having other choices, other paths it makes the actual experience a lot richer.
